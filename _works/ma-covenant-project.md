This research project systematically identifies and analyzes racially restrictive covenants—one of the most widespread mechanisms through which housing discrimination shaped residential patterns and household wealth inequality in the United States. Analyzing historical property deeds from three Massachusetts counties (Northern Middlesex, Worcester, and Norfolk), the research employs a Large Language Model (LLM)-based classifier supplemented by manual verification to identify, map, and quantitatively analyze patterns of racial exclusion embedded in property records.

The project reveals how racial categories evolved within covenant language over several decades—from vague prohibitions against "disorderly" persons targeting Irish immigrants, to explicit exclusions of "Irish" and "Negro" occupants, ultimately culminating in restrictions limiting occupancy exclusively to "Caucasian" individuals. It uncovers the pivotal role of real estate developers in standardizing and distributing these exclusionary clauses during land subdivision, embedding racial hierarchies into the physical fabric of suburban Massachusetts communities.

By illuminating the social construction of race through property law and private practice, this research contributes to broader understandings of racial formation, property-based inequality, and the lasting effects of discriminatory housing policy. Findings will be made publicly accessible through a mapping tool, supporting policy efforts toward reparative lending and equitable reinvestment in historically marginalized communities.
